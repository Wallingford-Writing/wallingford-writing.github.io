Support

Found a bug, spotted a typo, or have an idea? We'd love to hear from you.

Get in touch

Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. It helps to tell us your device, which piece or screen you were on, and what you expected to happen. If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue.

Common questions

What ages is it for?

Writers of about 6 to 9. Every line a character says is spoken aloud. There is nothing to do against a clock and nothing to lose.

The reader said it didn't work. Did we do something wrong?

No. Every piece has one writer's choice with three options, and the ones that don't do the job are there to be tried. The reader says what happened, and only that one sentence goes back to be written again. An option already tried is set aside.

Check keeps pointing at the same line.

Nib gives the rule for the first line that still needs something. The first word of a sentence, every word of a name (both words of "Kite Hill") and the word I need a capital; nothing else does. Tap a word again to make it small. Then pick an end mark. Some sentences take a period or an exclamation mark; a question takes a question mark.

A spelling word is too hard.

Tap Hear it to listen again. Wrong letters go back to the tray and the right ones stay. After two tries the word shows faintly in the boxes to copy. It will come back later in the Word Jar.

Where is the Notebook?

It opens at the Word Stand once the first piece is pinned to the Corner Board. Pages save when you go back. A page left empty isn't kept.

What does Nib's look check?

Three things only: a sentence that starts with a small letter, a sentence with no end mark, and a small i on its own. It doesn't check spelling or ideas, it can be wrong about unusual sentences, and it never stops a page from being saved.

Can she change her signature?

Yes. The round arrow beside the signing box clears it, any time a piece is being pinned up. Every piece on the board shows the newest one.

We stopped in the middle of a piece.

Nothing is lost. The draft is saved after every sentence, and Keep writing at the Word Stand picks it up where it was.

How do I turn off the voice or the music?

The two buttons at the top of the title screen switch the spoken lines and the music (with the sound effects) on and off. The home button at the Word Stand goes back to the title screen.

The voice sounds robotic, or doesn't speak.

The lines are read by your device's own speech voice. Check that the device isn't muted and the volume is up. On iPhone and iPad, a better voice can be downloaded in Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices.

How do I move her writing to a new phone or tablet?

Everything is saved only on the device it was written on, and there is no way to move it yet. A full device backup and restore (iCloud or Google) may bring it along.

Does it need the internet?

No. Wallingford Writing works fully offline, even in airplane mode.

Terms

Wallingford Writing is a game about writing. It teaches a few habits of writers and a few rules of capitals and end marks; it is not a full curriculum and doesn't replace a teacher.

In the game, a grown-up goes along on every outing away from the playfield. Please do the same in real life, and ask before putting up a sign anywhere that isn't yours.

The neighborhood in the game is inspired by real public places in Seattle's Wallingford neighborhood. The characters, the school and every business are made up, and any resemblance to real people or businesses is a coincidence.
